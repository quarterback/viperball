# COACH-CASE-01 coaching policy audit

November 27, 2026. Scope covers the college game, season and dynasty. Findings refer to `main` at `ad32c9e`. The ten cases describe football practices to translate into choices available to fictional Viperball coaches. Their examples supply scenarios and tradeoffs. Ratings, frequencies and effect sizes require Viperball evidence. [Research casebook](https://github.com/quarterback/viperball/blob/ad32c9e/docs/football_coaching_casebook_2026-11-27.md)

## What the engine already does

Two coaches can already call different games from the same school. An actual caller's persistent signature changes formations, families, kick pass subtypes, defensive posture or special teams decisions. Authority and familiarity affect how much of that preference reaches the field. A custom book changes the catalog packages available to its team, and CPU teams can already adopt a compatible book. Personnel fit affects recruiting boards and staff choices. These are working sources of variation to build on. [Coaching spec](../COACHING_SPEC.md) · [Playbook Cookbook review](aar_2026-11-27_playbook_cookbook_review.md)

The largest gaps are a shared practice budget, reusable sequences that respond to an opponent's observed behavior, and a program plan that connects the decisions of its units and weighs immediate roster needs against development. Familiarity currently rises for every installed package. A narrow book therefore has no practice advantage over a broad one. An existing defensive adjustment, a single snap play package and a formation drawing each model something different from a named sequence of executable calls.

## Shared policy contract and ownership

This contract specifies what later packages share. Existing structures supply the initial values and neutral behavior for older saves. Define the persisted schema when the first consumer lands.

| Layer | Owner | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Ability | `coaching`, `coach_career` and `position_staff` | Ratings and role readiness describe what a coach can execute, evaluate or teach. Preserve existing growth and technical development envelopes. |
| Tactical philosophy | `ai_coach`, `coach_tactics`, `coach_school_continuity` and the catalog | The school is the program's base system. The coach card carries a persistent, versioned interpretation that follows a job change. Generate variations by coach identity with bounded correlations and preserve existing signatures. |
| Personnel preferences | `coach_recruiting_fit`, `position_staff`, `recruiting` and `transfer_portal` | Describe which skills, positional roles, depth gaps and development horizons the coach values. Existing markets and budgets enforce feasibility. Preferences affect rankings and decisions while player ratings and eligibility remain authoritative inputs. |
| Decision behavior | `coach_career` for authority, existing domain selectors for decisions | Persist a versioned coach policy for how to choose in context, including conditional tempo, plan breadth and risk. Resolve authority for each decision. Use policy as bounded inputs to the active selector. |
| Readiness | `team_installation` and `playbook_catalog` | The team owns the installed revision, permitted catalog concepts and practiced familiarity. Portable book content and personal preferences travel separately from the learning retained by the team. |

**Request.** The owning selector provides decision kind and phase, team and unit, actual authorized actor, policy version, applicable installed book and plan revision, visible game or career context, and its available options. Use stable option IDs. For game decisions, distinguish known rule state, own personnel readiness, and observations of an opponent with their sample and source. The offensive family follows the visible formation and defensive commitment in the current engine. A new tactic may read only declared visible aspects of that commitment at its permitted phase. Keep future calls, private opponent internals and outcome RNG outside the policy inputs. Audit existing fatigue and pressure inputs before exposing them as new coach observations.

**Choice and fallback.** The owner applies legality, availability, budget, installation, familiarity and authority checks for its domain. The policy may score or prioritize the surviving options and choose via the existing selector. Return the intended choice, its reason and any relevant weights. Preserve the existing base choice when a plan is missing, interrupted or has no eligible step. On field, the engine rules decide what is possible, the caller chooses among legal concepts, and the designated player may resolve a read after the call. An outcome belongs to the resolver and may diverge from the intent through the existing contest, penalties or a failed read.

**Lifetime.** Store policy and stable revisions with the coach or team record that owns them. Older saves use existing decisions for missing policy fields, with no synthetic practice or learning history. Preserve version 1 Cookbook import and snapshots. Runtime sequence progress and observations have explicit scope and expiration. A possession script clears on actual loss of possession. Define pauses and resumptions around penalties, injuries and quarter breaks using the existing possession boundary. Handle a change of caller at an explicit boundary and preserve the historical attribution of prior choices.

**Trace.** Extend offensive and defensive call records, special teams events, installation history, recruiting and portal histories, and staff and learning events. Attach the decision ID or play, team, actual caller ID or other authorized actor, policy revision, available options, intended and actual choice, reason, and a compact set of context and weights when available. Join results from the existing ledger. Record package selection, actual formation and action, a player read, and its result as distinct facts. Capture a pause, skipped step or fallback with its reason. A play with a penalty can have a selected call and no counted execution.

The contract preserves current six down and kick modes, weather, fatigue, Delta, bonus possession and legal restart and return paths. Outcome accounting remains in the game ledger with touchdowns worth 9, snap kicks 5, field goals 3, safeties 2, pindowns 1 and bells 0.5. Coach policy changes decisions through the existing selectors and contests.
