# Coaching career paths and position staff

Approved November 25, 2026. Follow-up to CCH-08/CCH-09 and Step 7 (PR #383). Independent of concurrent playbook changes.

## Goals and design

- Special teams is a durable specialty and also a pathway into OC/DC/HC work. At initial assignment target 65% career specialists, 25% interested in OC/DC, 10% interested in HC. These are durable preferences, not annual transition probabilities. Secondary discipline and relevant experience govern candidacy.
- For OC/DC, target 75% single-discipline, 20% with a secondary discipline, 5% tactical generalists. Aim for roughly 5–10% of coordinators actually serving on both sides over a 20–30-year career. Remove the existing 30% per-role random eligibility on each portal entry. Preserve general skills, reputation and tactical identity on a move, but limit unfamiliar-role authority until experience is gained. Do not force promotion or crossover.
- Introduce up to six named position assignments: Zerobacks, Backs & Vipers, Offensive Line, Defensive Line, Keepers & Coverage, Kicking & Returns. Smaller programs can cover assignments with coordinators; larger programs may employ dedicated coaches. A coordinator can hold a combined assignment; do not count them twice in salary, experience or training.
- Position coaches have persistent identity, teaching and recruiting abilities, technical specialty, career experience and ambition. Position coaching is a meaningful permanent job as well as a route from former player to coordinator. A veteran specialist may out-earn an inexperienced coordinator at a smaller school.
- Effects accumulate off the field through attribute-specific development, scouting, recruitment, personnel evaluation, installation and mentorship, retention and continuity. Reallocate the existing coaching development allowance rather than stacking new blanket bonuses. No new live play-resolution multipliers from position coaches.
- Use existing coaching market, role histories, contracts, payroll, player development, recruiting and persistence. No separate shadow dynasty. Keep human senior staff decisions manual and make CPU appointments traceable. Avoid generating a new coach for every coordinator vacancy when qualified existing assistants are available.

## Implementation sequence

1. Persist career intent, secondary discipline and role qualifications; wire market eligibility/ranking, readiness and cross-role transition effects.
2. Add position assignments and named staff with tiered coverage, durable experience, contracts and promotion eligibility. Make departures/vacancies and former-player entry visible; preserve identity through save/load.
3. Connect technical instruction to existing bounded player-development budget. Use relevant expertise for recruit scouting/targeting and personnel evaluations; use existing installation and relationship mechanisms for continuity where evidence exists. No automatic ratings inflation.
4. Extend existing dynasty reports/tests to measure tenure, role transitions, specialist retention, promotion sources, generated fills, salaries, player skill growth, alumni entry and long-run talent distribution. Test save/load, one coach/one job, budget and human control. Use existing multi-seed longitudinal benchmark rather than new per-feature workflows.

## Acceptance and calibration

Targets above are proposed simulation inputs and long-run outcomes, not empirical facts or proof of balance. Report actual cross-discipline moves and career trajectories separately from eligibility. Compare against the existing longitudinal baseline. Do not claim long-run calibration until full runs complete. Document incomplete surfaces and scope explicitly in the PR/AAR.
