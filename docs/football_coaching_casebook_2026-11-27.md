# Football coaching casebook: translating coaching into Viperball
27 November 2026 | Research and design reference | College simulation first

This is a self-contained source packet for agents without internet search. Read the source summaries and implementation instructions here; links are provenance, not prerequisites for using the packet. This is not a list of real coaches to insert into the fictional league. Use the behaviors as ingredients for generated coaches. Evidence describes football; all Viperball mappings, proposed policy parameters and test scenarios are design hypotheses, not historical measurements. Do not assign numerical ratings to a real coach from an anecdote.

## Case 1 — Lane Kiffin: speed, exploitation, and roster churn
Evidence:
1. [Ole Miss weekly press conference, 12 Oct 2020](https://olemisssports.com/news/2020/10/12/football-lane-kiffin-weekly-press-conference). Kiffin explained that his offense can have the next call ready before officials spot the ball; the defense is occupied communicating and aligning. That is a specific mechanism for tempo advantage, not a claim that speed always wins.
2. [Saban on Kiffin, SEC media days 2021](https://www.saturdaydownsouth.com/news/college-football/everything-nick-saban-said-at-sec-media-days/); [Saban 2022 radio interview reported by On3](https://www.on3.com/news/nick-saban-opens-up-about-lasting-impact-lane-kiffin-had-alabama-offense-coordinator-ole-miss-bryce-young/). Saban recruited Kiffin to help research and adopt spread/RPO concepts; both previously had pro-style backgrounds. Alabama later adjusted to Bryce Young's strengths. Kiffin did not invent the RPO and was not born with an immutable spread identity.
3. [Ole Miss signing day, 21 Dec 2022](https://olemisssports.com/news/2022/12/21/football-lane-kiffin-signing-day-press-conference); [13 Dec 2022 press conference](https://olemisssports.com/news/2022/12/13/football-lane-kiffin-press-conference). Kiffin described an incomplete roster until transfers arrived, concurrent recruiting/practice demands, and reuse of assistants from FAU. Portal access changes acquisition and retention work; staff continuity can ease installation.
Translation: Stampede / Mismatch / Slick 'n' Slide / Shock and Awe are candidate schools, not a mandatory combination. Favor spread_motion or vertical_attack where roster fits; target Viper separation, Zeroback awareness/accuracy, lateral threats and depth. A fast coordinator preselects a small legal follow-up menu after an advantageous result, repeats a favorable matchup until the defense adjusts, and uses tempo only when the next call, personnel and fatigue permit it. Prefer near-term transfer help at an actual depth/skill gap if the existing league market supports it; compare eligibility years, continuity and signing risk with a high-school recruit. No automatic signing or universal portal advantage.
Gap: existing signature/tempo/recruiting fit can express part of this. Conditional possession-level acceleration, substitution/readiness constraints, matchup memory and explicit transfer-versus-development horizon need code audit; implement only missing mechanics. Never model a defense as unable to act without a legal, evidenced readiness mechanism.
Costs: fast failed possessions expose own defense; thin roster can tire; fast decisions may sacrifice diagnosis; high churn risks familiarity. Metrics: pace conditional on prior result and state, follow-up family mix, repeated matchup attempts, early exit possessions, fatigue, incoming transfer contribution, installation loss. Test same roster against thin vs deep Rotation defense, then depleted own Zeroback. Do not assume historically fixed sixth-down aggression or assign a probability from football fourth downs.

## Case 2 — Bill Parcells: rebuilding, coordination, and complementary football
Evidence:
1. [Giants retrospective, Belichick's game plan](https://www.giants.com/news/belichick-s-gameplan). Super Bowl XXV defensive staff prioritized coverage against Buffalo despite a season-long run-defense identity, accepted rushing concessions, and supported a possession-oriented offense. Buffalo held the ball 19:27 and converted one of eight third downs; Giants won 20–19. One game is a case, not a causal estimate.
2. [NFL retrospective on the 1990 staff](https://www.nfl.com/news/parcells-1990-coaching-staff-was-ultimate-super-group-09000d5d80631278). Staff accounts distinguish Parcells' oversight, Erhardt's offense, Belichick's defense and junior assistant contributions. Preparation problems led to staff revisions; the offense used the line and short passes against Buffalo's defense.
3. [Contemporary LA Times report, 22 Jan 1991](https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1991-01-22-sp-762-story.html). Reports patient tempo but also willingness to run on fourth down; avoid equating conservative tempo with never taking late-down risk. [Washington Post, 21 Jan 1993](https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/sports/1993/01/22/parcells-finds-new-job-as-coach-of-patriots/dc7ef97a-55b3-4bf7-b944-17894b878fa6/) documents significant personnel authority in the New England rebuild.
Translation: HC coordinates offense, defense and ST, selects reliable teachers and specialists, evaluates personnel by functional roles, and chooses a strategy for the whole matchup. Against elite explosive offense, an Ironclad/Ball Control offense might use possession and field position while a Drift/Lockdown defense accepts small advances in exchange for reduced explosive exposure. Against a weaker defense with excellent own offense, do not force slow tempo or a power identity. Keep OC and DC attribution separate.
Gap: staff authority, continuity, school selection and packages exist; inspect whether HC can coordinate weekly objectives across units and personnel board priorities by depth. Potential shared plan object: opponent threats, tolerated concessions, target possession/territory behavior, specific situational goals, unit authority. Evaluate feasible actions; do not grant possession automatically.
Costs: low possessions amplify individual mistakes and can leave insufficient clock when behind; defensive concessions may become scoring chances; roster/staff reconstruction takes seasons. Metrics: possessions allowed, score/territory tradeoff, sixth-down decisions by distance and clock, actual delegated calls, year-over-year depth and continuity. Test strong and weak inherited offenses separately, plus a trailing late-game state. No artificial comeback or rebuild bonus.

## Case 3 — Bill Walsh: diagnostic scripting
Evidence: [Belichick's 24 Oct 2002 press conference](https://www.patriots.com/news/bill-belichick-press-conf-transcript-10-24-2002-123706), a firsthand practitioner account of Walsh's scripting legacy. Opening plays target personnel and elicit defensive responses to formations; later calls use observed patterns. [9 Dec 2002](https://www.patriots.com/news/bill-belichick-press-conf-transcript-12-09-2002-125356) clarifies that short-yardage situations can interrupt a script. This is corroborating testimony, not Walsh's own playbook. Walsh's Finding the Winning Edge is a follow-up book lead, not a source consulted in this pass.
Translation: East Coast/Mousetrap and short kick-pass concepts fit. Script early probes: inside run, perimeter run, quick kick pass, Viper motion; choose only legal installed concepts. Record defensive alignment/commitment and result, update opponent-response estimates, then choose counter. A third/sixth-down constraint, injury or score/clock priority pauses script; do not blindly advance queue. 
Gap: Cookbook supports a single recipe, not ordered sequences; add optional ordered/conditional group with stable revision and logged skip reasons. In-game opponent observations must be distinct from CCH-08 offseason learning. Costs: early probes can waste opportunities; small samples mislead; defensive deception can bait a counter. Test opponent that changes alignment after drive one; log script progress, skips, counter rationale and value; compare against equally familiar unscripted offense.

## Case 4 — Mike Leach: finite practice, deep mastery
Evidence: [2022 SEC media days transcript](https://www.saturdaydownsouth.com/news/college-football/everything-mike-leach-said-from-the-podium-at-sec-media-days/). Leach described replacing a play when adding one to preserve practice and execution time; technique and small variations evolve. [Spokesman-Review, 29 Aug 2019](https://www.spokesman.com/stories/2019/aug/29/since-mike-leachs-arrival-on-the-palouse-washington-state-has-gone-all-in-on-the-air-raid-offense/) includes quarterback accounts of the importance of repetitions and consequences of missed practice.
Translation: Shock and Awe / East Coast with a compact family of quick, territory and deep kick-pass concepts, varied formations/tags where supported. Allocate a finite amount of practice to fewer concepts, keeping core familiarity high. Keep line protection, Zeroback accuracy and receivers' timing in recruitment/development. Do not confuse Air Raid with only deep throws or unconditional hurry-up.
Gap: package caps and fixed growth exist; investigate a conserved practice budget and concept-level familiarity rather than simply granting high familiarity. A wider repertoire has lower reps per concept, but greater counter coverage. Distinguish concept from formation variation so tags don't magically create mastery. Costs: coverage can key on core concepts; poor kick-pass personnel or injuries may be hard to compensate for. Test narrow/deep vs wide/shallow with same total practice time and matched opponents; track actual repertoire entropy, reps, familiarity, turnovers and efficiency against Lockdown and pressure.

## Case 5 — Bill Belichick: prepare contingencies from a practiced repertoire
Evidence: [13 Sep 2002](https://www.patriots.com/news/bill-belichick-press-conf-transcript-09-13-2002-123071) describes selecting weekly from existing plays based on matchups. [6 Dec 2002](https://www.patriots.com/news/bill-belichick-press-conf-transcript-12-06-2002-125626) discusses defensive priorities by situation, observation of the opponent's opening plan, in-game adjustments and complementary answers if the first strategy is taken away. [26 Sep 2003](https://www.patriots.com/news/bill-belichick-press-conf-transcript-9-26-03-125711) warns against an unfamiliar counter the team cannot execute. [1 Nov 2002](https://www.patriots.com/news/bill-belichick-press-conf-transcript-11-1-2002-123456) describes phased practice: early downs first, then third down, then red zone and two-minute work, with reviews.
Translation: any defensive school, broad well-practiced menu of compatible commitments, weekly prioritization of opponent's most dangerous scoring/advance routes and acceptable concessions. Against lateral teams prioritize Swarm-like pursuit; against dangerous bombs prioritize coverage; use actual packages, never switch school labels merely to express a game plan. Prioritize six-down equivalents, kick defense and Delta situations in weekly practice. A second meeting with the same opponent may use prior evidence but must allow opponent adjustment.
Gap: weekly counter-school bias and commitments already exist. Audit whether coach can select a subset of familiar packages per opponent, allocate practice by situation, retain uncertain scouting estimates and change plan within game without changing school. Need not build a new defensive resolver. Costs: breadth consumes practice; scouting errors and injuries undermine plans; conceding the wrong play family is costly. Test same DC vs three stylistically different teams, same roster and seeds. Measure week-to-week package distribution, retained identity, permitted alternatives and actual commitments (not just intended calls).

## Case 6 — Nick Saban: institutional continuity and strategic reinvention
Evidence: [2021 SEC media days](https://www.saturdaydownsouth.com/news/college-football/everything-nick-saban-said-at-sec-media-days/) and [2022 radio interview](https://www.on3.com/news/nick-saban-opens-up-about-lasting-impact-lane-kiffin-had-alabama-offense-coordinator-ole-miss-bryce-young/). Saban described deliberately moving beyond an established offensive philosophy after observing successful opponents, hiring a coordinator to help learn unfamiliar concepts, and later adapting to quarterback strengths. [Sports Illustrated, 2020](https://www.si.com/college/alabama/bamacentral/nick-saban-vs-lane-kiffin-the-once-architect-of-alabama-s-modern-offense-is-now-tasked-with-dismantling-it) reports shared research and implementation.
Translation: maintain strong program identity and succession while permitting evidence-supported evolution on one side of the ball. Hire a coordinator with a complementary capability that the HC lacks; keep successful parts of the inherited book; stage new installation. Allow upward learning (assistant to HC) with attributed shared work, not just mentor to assistant. Saban-era Alabama is not one immutable formation preference.
Gap: continuity, career histories, coordinator adoption and limited mentorship exist; audit direction of learning and conditions for HC-led school transition. Require sustained evidence, a feasible roster/staff plan and installation cost; do not bypass CCH-08 bounds with a free skill upgrade. Costs: delayed transitions, conflicting teaching, staff turnover, recruiting mismatch. Test long dynasty with changing coordinator and personnel strength; compare continuity, changed school, retained concepts and role attribution. No guaranteed titles or prestige acceleration.

## Case 7 — Paul Johnson: read-based specialization
Evidence: [Navy, 27 Dec 2003](https://navysports.com/news/2003/12/27/Transcript_Of_Paul_Johnson_s_Comments_Following_Practice_On_Saturday_Dec_27.aspx). Johnson explains deliberately reading unblocked defenders to create a numbers advantage and varying passing use to suit personnel. [Georgia Tech, 29 Sep 2009](https://ramblinwreck.com/news/2009/09/29/paul-johnson-press-conference-quotes-15) distinguishes actual option reads from predetermined handoffs. [Navy, 26 Oct 2004](https://navysports.com/news/2004/10/26/transcript_of_comments_by_navy_head_football_coach_paul_johnson_following_practice_on_tuesday_october_26_2004.aspx) describes limited trained checks to a favorable front. [Navy, 13 Dec 2004 practice comments](https://navysports.com/news/2004/12/14/transcript_of_paul_johnson_s_comments_following_practice_on_monday_dec_13.aspx) emphasizes execution, call timing and related concepts over secrecy.
Translation: Slick 'n' Slide / Ground Control / lateral concepts. Coach selects a legal option concept, trained Zeroback resolves a defender's commitment into a keep/handoff/lateral or legal kick-pass branch as supported. Recruit for awareness, handling, lateral skill, blocking and timing rather than pure overall. A robust core may need fewer opponent-specific changes but still uses counters.
Gap: run option families and defensive reads exist; verify whether branching is truly conditional on a defender or just a preselected family outcome. If absent, design explicit read and opposing commitment in existing resolver, with player error and legal constraints; no automatic yards. Costs: specialized roster, transition time, ball-handling failures, disciplined defense, late-game urgency. Test stacked versus spread commitments and inexperienced vs veteran Zeroback; record selected read, actual branch, defender and failure modes.

## Case 8 — Bill Snyder: development and a resource-constrained rebuild
Evidence: [K-State staff hiring, 7 Oct 2011](https://www.kstatesports.com/news/2011/10/7/5583eba9e4b06b726e666208_131478204150145024) describes hiring known or recommended teachers aligned with program values. [K-State goals, 21 Dec 2012](https://www.kstatesports.com/news/2012/12/21/5583eeb1e4b06b726e6668b5_131478232074122712) documents repeated reinforcement of improvement, responsibility and consistency. [Sports Illustrated, 28 Oct 2011](https://www.si.com/more-sports/2011/10/28/bill-snyder-kansas-state) reports two program revivals, lightly recruited high-school players and junior-college transfers, and Snyder's teaching approach. These are evidence of choices and program history, not a quantified morale effect.
Translation: prioritize under-recruited players with a fit to actual system and development runway, use legal experienced transfers strategically, retain and teach; hire known strong position teachers where budget permits. Invest in fundamentals and installation rather than buying a short-lived prestige boost. A low-prestige program should show a distinctive recruiting board and gradual improvement, not guaranteed success.
Gap: development allowances, teacher slots, fit, retention and continuity already exist; audit whether staff hiring distinguishes teaching competence, roster horizon and known working relationships. If junior-college transfer route is absent, do not invent an eligibility exception; use supported recruiting routes. Costs: slower first-year recovery, missed elite recruits, dependence on continuity, succession risk. Test low-prestige programs over 10+ seasons with matched resource constraints; measure initial board targets, roster turnover, technical growth and results conditional on talent. No generic culture multiplier.

## Case 9 — Kirk Ferentz: territory, special teams and patience
Evidence: [Iowa 15 Sep 2026 press conference](https://hawkeyesports.com/news/2026/09/15/kirk-ferentz-news-conference-transcript-09152026) describes an 11-minute fourth-quarter possession without explosive plays, patient territory control, and positive ST contribution. [24 Nov 2026](https://hawkeyesports.com/news/2026/11/24/kirk-ferentz-news-conference-transcript-11242026) discusses learning and judgment by a young punt returner and inconsistent young kicking personnel.
Translation: Ironclad / Ball Control, Drift/Fortress and Iron Curtain candidates. Coordinate kicking/return decisions with where the defense can play, protect possession, invest in Keeper/kicker/return technique and depth. Recognize half-point bells, pindowns, safeties and bonus possession costs; use full expected value when validated instead of transplanting NFL punt policy. A coach with territorial preferences should still pursue necessary scoring when behind.
Gap: STC tendencies and five schemes, game-state tactics and position teachers exist. Inspect whether HC chooses joint unit priorities and values territory in scoring decisions; analytics plan's empirical EP is the proper future dependency for quantitative claims. Costs: stalled possessions, missed kicks and low ceiling when needing rapid points; Delta restart rules may reduce value of a lead. Test matched teams in close/leading/trailing states; measure field-position starts, legal return choices, all-six-channel scoring, possessions and clock. Do not assume territorial strategy always wins in Viperball.

## Case 10 — Andy Reid: coordinated creativity and sequencing
Evidence: [Chiefs, execution vs design, 2017](https://www.chiefs.com/news/execution-vs-design-the-credit-debate-behind-the-chiefs-big-play-offens-19792060). Reid describes concepts practiced since camp, adding and reviewing new ones, and the importance of execution. Players describe practice against varied defensive looks. [Chiefs coordinators, 2015](https://www.chiefs.com/news/here-s-what-we-learned-from-the-coordinators-on-thursday-16479201) includes Doug Pederson explaining playcalling changes and between-series assistant suggestions. [The Ringer, 6 Feb 2025](https://www.theringer.com/2025/02/06/nfl/andy-reid-game-planner-opening-script-kansas-city-chiefs-super-bowl) interviews staff about collaboration on the weekly menu and Reid's final sequencing.
Translation: Ghost / Mismatch / Mousetrap; build paired plays that present the same observable formation but threaten different actions. Staff propose legal concepts; actual caller chooses order; keep novelty tied to players who can execute it. A head coach may temporarily delegate a unit and take it back, with actual caller recorded.
Gap: cookbook recipes and authority exist; audit ability to represent related same-look concepts, ordered conditional follow-ups, between-series suggestions and temporary caller transitions. Do not synthesize new routes from diagrams without resolver support. Costs: rehearsal demands, errors in complex concepts, predictable sequencing when counters are scouted. Test same formation followed by different legal concepts after opponent overcommitment, and same roster with narrow vs varied menu. Log actual caller, familiarity, sequence and defensive response.

## Cross-case design: distinguish decisions from skill and school
Do not create ten new schools or ten named fixed profiles. A coach can be a narrow-book tempo specialist, broad-book slow coach, strong staff builder with little playcalling interest, or a developmental coach who changes systems. Compose persistent preferences and context policies independently of ability; allow plausible correlations and variation, not determinism. A great tactician can be a weak recruiter. A small-school teacher can outperform a famous hire in the right context. The names above are reference cases, not the fictional population distribution.

Candidate decisions and common contract (proposal, not existing fields):
| Decision | Inputs available to coach | Observable output | Existing owner / gap to audit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Repertoire construction | school, personnel, prior book, time | installed concepts, replacements | team_installation/catalog; finite practice allocation |
| Weekly preparation | prior games, opponent scout, familiarity, clock/field context | prioritized packages, reps, concessions | weekly plan; joint unit priorities |
| Live playcall | actual caller, legal actions, down, distance, score, clock, observations | formation/family/subtype, reason | existing selector; conditional sequence |
| Execution read | selected legal concept, defender, player skill | branch, actual result | existing resolver; verify real read |
| Tempo | own/opponent fatigue, substitution eligibility, prior play | elapsed time, pace choice | engine; conditional pace |
| Staff | vacancies, budget, career histories, goals | hires, delegation, succession | coach_career/portal/roles |
| Personnel | roster horizon, skills, budget/eligibility, scheme | recruit ranking, offers, depth | recruiting_fit/position_staff; acquisition horizon |
| Learning | attributable results, confidence, shared seasons | bounded drift, retained/retired concepts | coach_learning; upward influence / live observation separately |

At each decision log the available alternatives, actual choice, caller ID, policy version, context, reasons/weights (where available), and result. The coach may use only information available before decision, not opponent hidden future choices or outcome RNG. Reuse current ledgers; avoid duplicating stats. Persist policy versions; old saves migrate neutrally. Never stack a second independent play caller or bypass package eligibility.

## Implementation work sequence (proposed; check active PRs and existing IDs first)
1. COACH-CASE-01 — Audit all current decision points against the table and case scenarios. For each mark live, metadata only, partial, absent or not applicable, with exact file/function/test evidence. Provide a single shared policy contract and ownership map. No refactor for its own sake.
2. COACH-CASE-02 — Repertoire and practice: compact/deep vs broad/shallow, explicit budget, familiarity, installation and paired recipes. Coordinate Cookbook work. Preserve old defaults unless migration approved. Acceptance: finite allocations, no free mastery, legal calls, save/load.
3. COACH-CASE-03 — Live sequencing and matchup response: script interruption, observable scout memory, complementary counters, conditional tempo where supported. Coordinate with offensive/defensive selector owners. Acceptance: equal roster matched traces, legal selection, no future information, actual-call attribution, sensible opponent-specific difference and failure cases.
4. COACH-CASE-04 — Program and staff: joint objectives, roster horizon, teaching, staff selection and succession, bounded upward learning if missing. Acceptance: actual hires/board/development differences with unchanged player-generation rules, realistic constraints, longitudinal persistence.
5. COACH-CASE-VAL — Integrated calibration, not a separate per-feature CI suite. Extend existing benchmarks, matched games and national dynasty workflow. Compare same school/roster with different policies, same policy with different rosters, and fresh hire vs established. Include weak opponent, strong opponent, fatigue, injury, weather, close/leading/trailing, Power Play/Penalty Kill/neutral, bonus possession, all six scores. Log decision signatures, tradeoffs, sample counts and uncertainty, not just win rate. Test multiple seeds and 10/20/30-season careers where practical; quantify CPU adoption and staff mobility. Check existing analytics development plan; use static EP only within its disclosed scope, do not label unsupported tactical value as causal. Report no effect and adverse effects. No win-rate target or forced rankings.

Parallel work: audit and contract first; research, scenario fixtures and offline studies can overlap. Once the shared interfaces are agreed, repertoire, live behavior and personnel/staff work can proceed on distinct files with one engine integration owner. No unapproved numerical tuning or automatic implementation from these examples. Integrate all compatible changes before final full-game acceptance. Maintain AAR with commands, seeds, baseline/candidate revisions, tests, limitations and artifacts. Code not changed by this casebook.

## Evidence limits and next research
This pass uses public press conference excerpts, practitioner accounts and reporting. Official team accounts may emphasize successes. It does not independently chart film, fit historical tendencies, establish causal performance effects, or review full books. Kiffin late-down aggression, coach-specific exact recruiting rates, Walsh's original script details, and Reid's play-design frequency require quantitative or primary-source follow-up before claiming historical rates. Do not rely on celebrity anecdotes as numerical priors. Useful next leads: Walsh's Finding the Winning Edge; Parcells' Finding a Way to Win; S. C. Gwynne's The Perfect Pass; coordinator clinic recordings, public play-by-play data and season-level roster histories. Those books were not consulted here. This packet provides enough grounded material to design and test fictional coaching policies without internet access.
