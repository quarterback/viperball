# Delta special-situations master coaching clinic
27 November 2026 | Implementation context for PR #399 and subsequent work

## Commission
Build modular, installable Power Play offense, defense against Power Play (PK defense), and backed-up Penalty Kill offense. These are specialized books within existing schools, not new universal base schemes, and must be callable in actual games. Do not return another research-only report. This supplement is self-contained for agents without internet search. All calls below are original coaching designs, not claims that another sport uses these precise plays.

## Evidence and coaching lessons
- USA Hockey, Mike Guentzel, https://www.usahockey.com/news_article/show/567732-special-teams-tactics-with-mike-guentzel : start with rehearsed entry/retrieval and dependable finishing routines, use movement to create local overloads, concentrate skilled distributors where they see several options, adjust when defenders take a threat away. Penalty killers can apply selective pressure rather than wait in a stationary shape. Viperball retains 11v11; local overloads redistribute real players, never add one.
- Hockey Canada HP1 2022 facilitator tasks, https://cdn-ca.aglty.io/bc-hockey/image-gallery/coach-page/2022_HP1%20-%20Seminar%20Post%20Tasks.pdf p8: separately train entries, offensive setups, recovery, situational restarts, defensive systems and evaluation. Apply to modular installation, not hockey manpower or shot percentages.
- Hockey coaching systems, https://overloadhockey.com/systems : umbrella, 1-3-1 and overload create distinct support geometries; box/diamond and pressure rotations respond to threat location. Source overview only. Use roles within 11v11 and legal depth; no hockey rule imports.
- Darrell Dickey, Glazier clinic https://www.glazierclinics.com/football-coach-resources/ramp-up-your-red/tight-zone-attack-with-tams-four-verts : distinguish red, tight and goal-line zones, anticipate different coverage/pressure as space compresses, maintain field-goal position and use width. Viperball has its own 9/5/3 finishing choices and legal kick-pass delivery; no forward hand pass.
- Joe Daniel red-zone defense https://www.joedanielfootball.com/blog/red-zone-defense : shorter field changes depth responsibilities and reaction time; adjust from base defense according to opponent and space.
- NFHCA toolkit https://nfhca.org/coach-education/new-coach-toolkit/ : penalty-corner routines and variations give a common practiced foundation. Study https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8366230/ discusses choosing variations based on defender strategy rather than only fixed advance selection. This is a rationale for rehearsed branches, not a measured Viperball advantage.
- Rugby league coaching notes https://www.lowermountainsjrlc.com.au/files/An%20Approach%20to%20Coaching%20RL.pdf p23: distinguish defending different field areas, width, marker assignments, recovery, communication and kick defense. Use six-down progression with Viperball's own first-down renewal. No rugby rucks or forced turnover after six if 20 gained.
- Soccer concepts and prior coaching clinic: https://www.fifatrainingcentre.com/en/practice/elite-sessions/in-possession/switching--play.php and https://www.fifatrainingcentre.com/en/game/game-analysis/in-possession/third-player-combinations.php ; concentrate threats before switching, maintain outlet and third runner. See master_coaching_clinic_cross_code_2026-11-27.md and football_coaching_casebook_2026-11-27.md. These principles are contextual, not literal imported formations.

## Viperball context and terminology
RULES.md: after ordinary score restart receiving team starts at 20 minus its lead (or plus deficit), clamped 1..80; configurable baseline and integer rounding in engine. Opening/half, safety, pindown, turnovers and bonus possessions are not automatically Delta advantages. Six downs to gain 20; 11v11, minimum five on line; single legal motion parallel/away, no forward hand pass, one kick pass per play, unlimited legal sideways/back laterals. All six scores unchanged (TD9, drop5, place3, safety2, pindown1, bell.5), score restart and miss rules unchanged. No extra player, no time-limited hockey penalty, no artificial scoring bonus.
Current stats use offensive drive delta_cost >0 = penalty_kill (leading team backed up), <0 = power_play (boosted), 0 neutral. Defense facing opposing boosted drive is colloquially PK defense, NOT the team's own offensive PK drive. Defense facing opposing backed-up drive is PP pressure defense; distinguish those in API/UI and reports. A PP drive can start at own21, not necessarily red zone; a neutral drive can reach goal line.
Examples baseline20: up15 -> own5; down5 -> own25; down20 -> own40; down45 -> opponent35; down60+ -> opponent20 cap. Late leading4 vs trailing opponent: a 5-point kick can change lead, so goal-line-only defense is inadequate. Leading8 late: touchdown prevention may dominate; still consider subsequent possessions, clock, bonus, scores and restart. Don't hardcode concede points.
Existing matched Delta study reports/delta_study/README.md (3,000 pairs, 6,000 games): games decided by <=9 rose 17.6 to 24.6%, Q4 trailing-team wins 9.4 to 13.8%, mean restart shift11.8, 44,204 restarts. Observational starting-yard regression .034 points/yard not causal play value. Do not use as individual package coefficient or turn Delta off in production.

## Offensive Power Play book
Notation: Z=Zeroback, V=Viper, H/W/S/F=four flankers, L1..5=line. Standard look has five line players and six legal backfield alignments. All hand transfers level/back. Each call must include a legal fallback. Codes below are proposed stable IDs; check collisions and equivalent installed concepts first. Existing families named as candidate compatibility; exact catalog tags to verify.

PP01 Advantage Entry (East Coast, Slick n Slide, Ball Control, Ground Control): boosted start own21–49; early/mid downs, especially modest boost. Split/tight; Z distributes quick kick pass or short legal lateral to H/W, use power/dive if wide pressure. Preserve possession, gain 20 then re-evaluate. Avoid acting desperate merely because trailing. Counter channel press or interior wall. Risk short gains, outlet INT. Existing family/subtype preference feasible now; actual lane needs channel model.
PP02 Umbrella (Boot Raid, East Coast, Six Shooter, Mismatch): boosted in viable scoring territory, usually downs3–6. Five L protect; Z central/deep; V interior behind line; W/F wide, H/S staggered trailing outlets. Read visible pressure: compact front -> wide quick/territory kick pass or sweep; wide shell -> interior run; legal kick gate and useful score -> existing snap kick; pressure high -> short safe outlet or counter. Actual kick option must respect clock/range and personnel. Kicking/dual Z, receiving/hybrid V. Counter PK02 diamond, PK03 channel lock. Cost longer release, loss of central protection if moving blockers. A coarse package plus finish policy now; geometry/role reads later.
PP03 Overload (Lateral Spread, Ghost, Mismatch, Chain Gang): boosted mid/launch zone, early/middle down. W/S/V threaten one side after legal snap, H/F retain opposite support; attack strong-side local advantage through legal lateral chain or carry; if actual defenders concentrate, use reverse/backward lateral or single kick pass to weak-side receiver. If no space, keep ball; no guaranteed defender drift. Distributor Z, decoy/hybrid V. Counter PK03/04. Risk extra handling and exposed weak-side support. Need explicit channel/participant state for real switch; generic lateral bias alone not full implementation.
PP04 Double Threat (Slick n Slide, Ghost, Stampede): same split look supports Z carry, legal lateral to H, or single kick pass; snap-kick finish only through existing legal policy. If actual front commits to kicker and run lane available use run; if wide containment threatens runner, quick outlet. Dual Z, hybrid V. Counter contain without excess rush. Costs pressure and execution complexity. No new multi-kick action.
PP05 Five-Nine (Boot Raid, Six Shooter, Ground Control): launch through compression, especially downs4–6. Preserve plausible nine-point finish while taking useful 5 when distance, clock, kicker, defensive risk, miss consequences and remaining downs justify; 3 outside comfortable snap range if legal/useful. Prefer short advancement when likely to earn new set. Use existing kick selector and future validated alternative-value model; don't assume static EP supports optimal choice. Counter PK05/06/07. Cost missed kick, stalled drive or opportunity cost.
PP06 Compression (Ground Control, Ghost, Mismatch, Slick n Slide): opponent15–1, all downs, power V and running/dual Z. Tight/split, interior carry or short kick pass to legal end-zone threat; use counter/edge only when compact front creates actual weakness. No illegal picks/wedges. Counter PK01/06. Risk tackle loss and compressed receiving windows. Separate tight zone from goal line.
PP07 Rapid Finish (Quick Strike, Shock and Awe, Stampede): boosted and late trailing, use actual clock and score needs; first legal advancement or scoring action with useful points, avoid pointless low-value kick where TD required. Existing pace/clock and kick policies, not automatic hurry-up advantage. Counter PK04/07. Costs fatigue, exposure to INT/bonus.
PP08 Second Shot (Boot Raid, Six Shooter, Ball Control): only after an actual missed snap kick where offense retains possession, same possession and next legal down. Re-evaluate kicker, pressure, new distance and score: repeat if viable; if pressure high, short advance/convert where useful; never repeat after possession-ending miss, block recovery turnover, pindown, or sixth-down turnover. Use prior resolved event not label or scheduled kick. Counter PK02/07. Costs declining downs and miss exposure. Existing kick_mode may need inspection for conflict; do not override hard rules.
Practice: run PP entry separately from launch and finish; rotate eligible lines, train same look with multiple outcomes, test at own25/40, opponent35/15/5 and capped opponent20 restart; different kicker strengths. No generic PP efficiency bonus.

## Penalty Kill defense against opposing PP
Use actual 11-player commitments with 5 DL, 4 backs and 2 Keepers or engine-valid variations. Labels describe responsibilities, not player counts imported from hockey. Allocate defenders once; a rusher cannot simultaneously cover. Keep kick return responsibilities available when legal. Existing defensive commitments already have posture, rusher count and focus; use them where equivalent.
PK01 Compact Box (Fortress, Drift): compressed field, interior tackle and shallow coverage; retain depth appropriate to single kick pass. Prefer fewer extra rushers. Concede outside advance; counter PP06/04. Distinct from automatic prevent.
PK02 Kicker Diamond (Keeper Hunt, Blitz Pack, Lockdown): viable opposing drop range, high-quality kicker, kick could materially change game. Assigned lead pressure and layered shallow/deep coverage, actual rusher budget; vary pressure with fatigue and legal context. Concede some interior run/quick outlet. Counter PP02/05/08. Existing kick shell/rush mechanics first, no invented simultaneous roles.
PK03 Side Lock (Swarm, Chaos, Shadow): visible strong-side concentration or repeated edge attempts; nearest cover presses boundary, another denies backward outlet, retain weak-side safety. Concede inside or opposite switch. Counter PP03/02. Real side-lock needs channel state; otherwise existing swarm/lateral trap equivalent, explicitly labeled coarse.
PK04 Rotating Shell (Lockdown, Rotation, Drift): against mobile/paired looks, communicate switch/stay, help and replace actual vacated assignments, maintain deep coverage. Concede some quick run, costs fatigue/awareness. Counter PP03/07. Requires assignment model; don't give generic coverage bonus.
PK05 Range Denial (Fortress, Blitz Pack, Shadow): boosted opponent outside viable drop range, keep advances short, prioritize preventing new set/comfortable range rather than chasing sacks at any cost. Controlled rush and interior support, concede some wide short yards. Counter PP01/05. Existing short wall/rush package; measure actual entry prevention.
PK06 Goal-Line Wall (Fortress, Lockdown): opponent5 or closer, nine points particularly consequential; commit actual line/short coverage, protect end-zone kick-pass threats, accept vulnerability to snap kick only if clock/score decision supports risk. Counter PP06/05. Existing goal-line package with PP context, no automatic concession.
PK07 Late-Down Surge (Keeper Hunt, Blitz Pack, Predator): fifth/sixth, observed kicking threat or conversion desperation, increase legal intended rush commitment with readiness and actual cover cost. Use existing rush/pressure/block contests. Counter PP05/07/08. Exposed draw/quick pass; no extra defender or guaranteed sack.
PK08 Takeaway Trap (Chaos, Predator, Swarm): observed lateral or kick-pass repetition, selective lane denial, actual INT/fumble resolver and bonus rules. Counter PP03/07. Risk explosive concession; avoid high-risk trap when score/clock favors simple stop. Existing chaos lateral trap/shadow robber can be adapted; true lane trigger requires channel model.
PK success isn't always zero points, nor always "prevent TD": report score distribution and actual remaining lead/clock, turnover, stop, kick attempts and pressure. Do not pretend all 3/5-point concessions are successful. Never deliberately allow score via probability bonus.

## Backed-up offensive Penalty Kill book (leading offense)
PKO01 Escape (Ironclad, Ball Control, East Coast): own1–10, first/middle downs, tight protection, reliable carry/quick outlet; no long lateral chain if unnecessary, safety risk. Counter pressure/inside wall.
PKO02 Secure Twenty (Ground Control, Slick n Slide): own11–25 or deeper after escape, target new set with safe distribution and clock; not automatically punt. Concede explosiveness.
PKO03 Territory Exit (Ironclad, Boot Raid): late down, legal kick/territory decision when conversion poor and score/clock permit; preserve existing no-punt-when-trailing policy, pindown/return risks. Never automatic punt.
PKO04 Counterpunch (Ghost, Mismatch, Quick Strike): when defense visibly overcommits to pressure at backed-up start, use counter/quick kick pass to available threat; only if personnel and bonus/security justify risk. Costs INT and exposure.
Defense against PKO may use existing pressure/safety containment as PP pressure defense (not same as PK defense above). Avoid two-way fatigue depletion before next opposing PP. Report safeties, bells, turnovers, escape past own20, renewed sets, points, clock and next-drive territory.

## Coordinator philosophy and unit allocation
Allow same school to install different mixes: kick-first (Umbrella/Five-Nine/Second Shot), touchdown-first (Compression/Double Threat), movement (Overload/Advantage Entry), tempo (Rapid Finish), conservative lead protector (Escape/Secure Twenty). Defense: pressure, layered coverage, territory denial, selective takeaway. Use actual caller, existing traits and skills; no new mandatory global coach type. Practice slots/reps and actual specialist fit determine execution. A Kicking ZB must not receive extra ratings; Receiving Viper shouldn't be compelled to play every snap. A coach can choose different legal lines for PP and PK when fatigue/rotation rules permit. Include STC consultation for kick coverage/return but keep OC/DC actual authority. Save and carry modules with installation, not new global RNG.
